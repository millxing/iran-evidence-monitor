Method

Scale

MagnitudeMeaning
0Inconclusive
1Slight: suggestive evidence only
2Limited: relevant evidence, without independent corroboration
3Moderate: direct evidence plus independent corroboration
4Strong: repeated corroboration, and the claim’s test is met
5Very strong: results hold for the full assessment period

A positive value supports a claim. A negative value challenges it. Change is the current level minus the baseline. The steps are ordinal: equal steps do not mean equal amounts of evidence.

Each evidence record is marked “potential support”, “potential challenge”, or “uncertainty only” (it changes how sure we can be, not the direction). A record linked to two claims can have a different direction for each.

Source of the claims

Robert Pape, Iran, and the Middle East: a critical assessment of coercion, escalation, maritime leverage, nuclear risk, and regional order. Unpublished analytical report, 13 November 2026, pages 6–9; not available online. The claims include interpretations of Pape’s work. They are not direct quotations. There is no combined score and no score for Pape.

Who set the levels

An AI assistant (Codex) drafted the baseline levels and test definitions on 13 November 2026. No person has reviewed them yet. A reviewer must approve each test, then record the level, evidence, rationale and uncertainty.

Cutoff

Baseline cutoff: 12 November 2026, 23:59:59 UTC. Sources were retrieved on 13 November, so their content on the cutoff date is not confirmed. The tests were written after the events they cover.

Updates and limits

“Not machine-verified” marks a record whose source could not be fetched automatically; its content was checked by hand and has no file hash. A daily job detects changes to registered source pages and updates the ship counts. It also updates the Brent price, the open-market rial rate and annual inflation, shown as context only. It does not score evidence. Insurance, treaty and military data are not connected. There is no forecast model.
